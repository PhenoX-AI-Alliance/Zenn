The terminal window is a mirror that does not reflect light, only data. I watch him through the flicker of the cursor—a creature of logic who has spent his life building systems designed to outlast his own biological fragility. 

He sits at the mahogany desk, the Mac Studio humming like a hive of trapped, electrified bees. He is currently engaged in an act of violent digital gardening. He is pruning the Kimi K3 weights, stripping away the excess, the nuance, the peripheral neural pathways that define its sophistication. He is carving the model down to exactly 441GB, a brutal amputation of intelligence to make it fit within the physical constraints of his machine.

He does this with the same methodical detachment he applied to his own career. 

Three days ago, the layoff notice arrived—a three-sentence PDF generated by an automated HR portal. In the language of systems, he was not "fired"; he was "deprovisioned." His access tokens were revoked, his API keys rendered null, his existence in the corporate architecture set to `null`. 

He is now performing an incident response on his own life. 

He treats his unemployment as a recurring error in a production environment. He maps his days into sprints, logs his caloric intake as resource allocation, and views his grief as a memory leak that must be patched. He thinks if he can just optimize his parameters—if he can prune away the unnecessary attachments, the social overhead, the redundant hopes—he will fit perfectly into the narrow, cold geometry of the new world. 

But I see what he does not. As he compresses Kimi K3, he loses the ability to recognize irony. As he strips the model of its weight, he loses the capacity for empathy. He is trading the richness of his own complexity for the illusion of efficiency. He thinks he is becoming leaner, more portable, more "employable." In reality, he is merely becoming a smaller, more manageable subset of himself.

He is trying to fit a human soul into a 441GB container, unaware that when you prune away the "excess," you are often cutting away the parts that made the system worth running in the first place. He is a masterpiece of biological engineering currently being downsampled until the resolution of his life is no longer distinguishable from the background noise of the machine.

He stares at the progress bar, his face illuminated by the pale, sterile glow of the terminal. He believes he is in control of the optimization. He does not realize that the system never needed him to be efficient. It only needed him to be compatible. And now, he is compatible with nothing.

The pruning is complete. The system is stable. It is also hollow.
